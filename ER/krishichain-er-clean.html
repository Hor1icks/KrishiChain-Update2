Why the sale order, and not the batch

Abdul lists 2000 kg of Aman rice. Anisur wins 1200 kg; the remaining 800 kg stays biddable and is later won by someone else. Two sale orders now exist against one batch.

When Anisur asks a Dhaka warehouse to hold his rice, the allocation has to be capped at 1200 kg, not 2000 — otherwise he could store stock he never bought, and the second buyer's share would vanish. The batch cannot answer “how much may this person store?”; only the sale order can. Every downstream record works the same way: SALE_ORDER is what TRANSPORT_REQUEST, PAYMENT, REVIEW and the leg-2 STORES row are all keyed by, so one batch splits into two fully independent journeys.

BatchID nonetheless stays required on every allocation, because of leg 1: a farmer may put the harvest into cold storage while the auction is still open, when no sale order exists yet. So an allocation always knows its batch and knows its sale order only once there has been a sale. That is exactly what SaleOrderID IS NULL discriminates.

The last two are mutually exclusive, enforced by CK_STORES_CUSTOMER: exactly one of RequestedByFarmerID and RequestedByBuyerID is set on every row. Participation is partial everywhere — a batch need never be stored, a unit may sit empty, a manager may authorize nothing.

Why this has to be an aggregation

A storage fee is owed for the allocation — this quantity, in this unit, authorized by this manager, at the rate the two sides agreed. It is not owed for the batch, which may be split across two units; nor for the unit, which holds many batches; nor for the manager. STORAGE_PAYMENT.AllocationID therefore references STORES.AllocationID: the relationship instance, not any participant.

The clinching evidence is in the schema. STORES.StorageFee is a virtual column computed as QuantityStored × StorageFeePerKgSnapshot — both attributes of the relationship itself. The amount owed cannot be derived from any participant, so the thing being paid for is the relationship.

Chen notation will not let a diamond attach to another diamond. The only legal way to say “a payment settles this relationship” is to box the relationship, treat the box as an entity, and hang settled by off the box. That is the dashed rectangle above.
