Part one

The journey

Ten stops, in the order they actually happen in the running system. Every stop opens with the path it walks — which table leads to which — so you can always see where you are before reading a word. All 28 relations appear exactly once, at the moment the story first needs them.

The backbone — everything else hangs off this one line

STOP 01Everyone

Five kinds of people, one kind of account

splits USERS→ FARMER· BUYER· ADMIN_STAFF· STORAGE_MANAGER· TRANSPORT_PERSONNEL
also USERS→USER_PHONE

Abdul Karim signs up as a farmer — but before he is a farmer, he is a user.

Five kinds of people use KrishiChain. They all need the same basic details: a name, a date of birth, an address, an email, a password. Those go in one table, USERS.

Each role then gets a small table for what only that role needs. A farmer has an NID. A buyer has a trade licence. A driver has a driving licence.

The small table reuses the same ID number. User 4 and farmer 4 are the same person. There is no second ID to keep in sync.

One person can have several phone numbers. A single column can only hold one value, so phone numbers move out into USER_PHONE.

USERSUSER_PHONE FARMERBUYER ADMIN_STAFFSTORAGE_MANAGER TRANSPORT_PERSONNEL
Construct 1 — total, disjoint specialization

Total means there is no user who is none of the five. Everybody is exactly one of them.

Disjoint means nobody is two of them at once. If Abdul also wants to buy, he makes a second account.

What we rejected One big USERS table with a role column and every role's extra fields added to it. It looks simpler, but then four out of five rows have an empty NID — so you can never mark NID as required, and you can never make it unique either. Separate role tables let each role keep its own rules.
STOP 02Farmer

A farm, a crop, and 5,000 kg of Aman rice

route FARMER→ FARM→ HARVEST_BATCH→ CROP→ CROP_CATEGORY
listed at HARVEST_BATCH→VIRTUAL_ARAT
loops VIRTUAL_ARAT→VIRTUAL_ARAT (parent arat)

He registers his land, then posts what came off it — and picks which arat to sell through.

Abdul registers his land. That is one FARM row, and it belongs to him and nobody else.

He then posts what he harvested: a HARVEST_BATCH of 5,000 kg of Aman rice. The batch points at three things — the farm it came from, the CROP it is, and the VIRTUAL_ARAT he wants to sell through.

Every crop sits inside a CROP_CATEGORY. Rice is a cereal. His asking floor of ৳34.00 has to be at least the crop's base price of ৳32.00, which is rule BR-09.

Arats form a chain of command: a district hub reports to a divisional hub, which reports to the national one. Instead of one table per level, one column does the whole job — ParentAratID points at another row in the same table.

The batch stores three numbers: total, reserved and sold. Available is not stored. Oracle works it out on the spot, so it can never disagree with the other three.

FARMCROP_CATEGORY CROPVIRTUAL_ARAT HARVEST_BATCH FARMER
Construct 2a — recursive relationship: supervises

One entity, two roles: the same table is both the parent and the child. So the diamond loops back to the box it came from.

The benefit is that the depth of the hierarchy becomes data. Add an upazila level and you insert rows — you do not change the schema.

Also decided here There is no AUCTION entity. An auction would have had exactly one row per batch, with no key and no life of its own. That is not an entity, it is a few extra columns. So the bidding window sits on HARVEST_BATCH, and bids point at the batch directly.
STOP 03Farmer + Storage manager

The rice goes into cold store before anyone bids

owns WAREHOUSE→STORAGE_UNIT (weak — needs its warehouse)
route HARVEST_BATCH+ STORAGE_UNIT+ STORAGE_MANAGER→ STORES→ STORAGE_PAYMENT

Unit 1 of Bogura Cold Storage — and a named manager is the one who authorised it.

A WAREHOUSE is split into units. Each unit has a number, a size and a status.

"Unit 1" on its own means nothing. Every warehouse starts numbering at 1, so there are five different unit 1s in the database. A unit is only identified once you also say which warehouse it is in. That is what makes it a weak entity.

Putting the rice into a unit involves three parties, not two: the batch, the unit, and the manager who approved it. All three go into one row of STORES.

Then the fee gets paid — 5,000 kg at ৳7.50/kg, so ৳37,500. It is paid for the allocation. Not for the batch, not for the unit, not for the manager. So STORAGE_PAYMENT points at the whole STORES row.

WAREHOUSESTORAGE_UNIT STORESSTORAGE_PAYMENT HARVEST_BATCHSTORAGE_MANAGER
Constructs 3a, 4a & 5a — ternary, weak entity, aggregation

Why one diamond with three legs, instead of two ordinary diamonds? Split it into "batch is in unit" plus "manager approved batch" and you can no longer tell which placement a manager approved. If a batch goes into two units, approved by two managers, the question becomes unanswerable. Keeping the three together keeps the answer.

Why the dashed box? A relationship is not allowed to connect to another relationship. But the payment really is for the allocation as a whole. So you draw a box around the allocation and connect to the box. That box is aggregation.

Frequently asked "Why is there no diamond between HARVEST_BATCH and STORAGE_UNIT?" — there is one, and it is STORES. The manager is simply a third leg of that same diamond, not a separate fact.
STOP 04Buyer

Three buyers, and one of them keeps raising

route BUYER→ BID→ HARVEST_BATCH
loops BID→BID (the bid it beat)

Each new bid points back at the bid it beat — the outbid chain is in the data, not in a log file.

A BID is a price per kg, a quantity and a time.

Tanvir in Dhaka opens at ৳34.50. Mizanur in Chattogram beats it with ৳35.25. Anisur in Sylhet wins with ৳36.00.

Each new bid records which bid it beat, in PreviousBidID. At the same moment, the old bid is marked OUTBID. Both changes happen in one transaction, so the two can never disagree with each other.

Five rules are checked on this one insert: the bid must beat the floor and the current highest, fit inside the quantity still available, meet the farmer's minimum lot size, not come from the farmer himself, and arrive before the window closes.

BID BUYERHARVEST_BATCH
Construct 2b — recursive relationship: outbids

Same idea as the arat hierarchy, different shape. An arat has many children, so that one is a tree. A bid beats exactly one earlier bid, so this one is a chain.

What we rejected A separate OUTBID table holding pairs of bid IDs. It would have exactly one row per bid and no information of its own — an extra join to learn something the bid could have told us directly.
STOP 05Farmer

The award — the one place the model gets interesting

route ( BUYER→ BID→ HARVEST_BATCH )→ SALE_ORDER
note the whole bracket is one participant — that is the aggregation

A sale order is not created by a buyer, or by a batch, or by a bid. It is created by all three, together.

Abdul accepts Anisur's ৳36.00 bid for 4,000 kg. That is a ৳144,000 order, and the remaining 1,000 kg stays on sale.

Now ask a simple question: what created this order?

Not the buyer — buyers exist without buying anything. Not the batch — plenty of batches go unsold. Not even the bid on its own, because a bid only means something as this buyer, on this batch, at this price. It was all three together.

ER notation will not let you draw a line from a relationship to another relationship. So you draw a dashed box around the whole thing and treat the box as a single participant. That is aggregation.

SALE_ORDER BIDBUYERHARVEST_BATCH
Construct 5b — aggregation (decision D-5)

This is also the clearest place to point at total vs partial participation, because both appear on the same line.

The order's end is a double line: no order can exist without a winning bid. The bid's end is a single line: most bids lose, and a losing bid is still a perfectly good bid.

What we rejected Just joining SALE_ORDER to BID with an ordinary line. The database columns would be identical — but the diagram would then be claiming the bid alone explains the order, which is not true. This is the one place where the ER diagram says something the SQL cannot.
STOP 06Buyer + Storage manager

Straight to my warehouse, or into cold store first?

route SALE_ORDER→ STORES→ STORAGE_UNIT (second leg, buyer's side)

Same ternary, second row — the buyer's storage leg reuses the construct we already built.

A batch can be in storage twice in its life. Once before the sale, paid by the farmer. Once after the sale, paid by the buyer.

Both are the same kind of fact — a batch, in a unit, approved by a manager — so both are rows in the same STORES table. The second one just also records which sale order it belongs to.

This is the payoff for modelling storage as one ternary. A workflow that arrived after the diagram was drawn needed no new table and no new construct.

STORESSALE_ORDER STORAGE_UNITBUYER
State, not extra tables

An allocation is a negotiation, not an order. It moves from proposed, to accepted, to release requested, to finished — and it can also be countered, rejected or cancelled.

All of that is one status column with a CHECK constraint. No state has any field the others lack, so no state earned its own entity.

STOP 07Transport personnel

A truck, a driver, and one trip

route SALE_ORDER→ TRANSPORT_REQUEST (exactly one per order)
claim TRANSPORT_REQUEST+ VEHICLE+ TRANSPORT_PERSONNEL→ ASSIGNED_TO

Who drove which vehicle on which trip is one fact — so it is one diamond with three legs.

Awarding the bid also creates a TRANSPORT_REQUEST. One per order, never two.

A driver then claims it. That claim needs three facts at the same time: which trip, which truck, which driver. So ASSIGNED_TO is a second ternary, built exactly the same way as STORES.

Split it into two separate pairs and you lose the answer to "who was driving which truck". Same problem, same solution — which is the point: the same shape of real-world fact got the same construct.

TRANSPORT_REQUESTVEHICLE ASSIGNED_TO TRANSPORT_PERSONNELSALE_ORDER
Construct 3b — ternary: ASSIGNED_TO

One difference from STORES worth knowing: a trip has at most one active assignment, but a truck and a driver each pick up many over time. The rule that stops a duplicate is the UNIQUE on all three columns together, not the primary key.

STOP 08Buyer → Farmer

Money moves, directly

route SALE_ORDER→ PAYMENT→ BUYER· FARMER
gated by TRANSPORT_REQUEST must say DELIVERED first

No commission, no escrow — the arat is a marketplace, not a middleman taking a cut.

Money goes straight from the buyer to the farmer. The arat takes no cut and holds nothing in the middle.

An order can be paid in parts, so one order can have several PAYMENT rows.

Two rules guard it. You cannot pay more than the order total. And you cannot pay before the goods are marked delivered.

The first rule has to add up all the earlier payments on that order — and Oracle will not let a normal trigger read the table it is writing to. So this one is a compound trigger: the delivery check runs per row, the total check runs once at the end.

PAYMENT SALE_ORDERBUYERFARMER
A deliberate denormalisation, if asked

You could work out the buyer and the farmer by following the order back through the bid and the batch. We store them anyway.

A payment record should say who paid whom at the time it happened, without depending on a four-table join that later changes could answer differently.

STORAGE_PAYMENT stays a separate table for a related reason: different payer, different receiver, different rules. Merging the two would put storage fees under rules written for sale money.

STOP 09Buyer + Admin

Rate the deal, or dispute it

review SALE_ORDER→ REVIEW(1:1 — one per order)
dispute SALE_ORDER→ COMPLAINT→ ADMIN_STAFF(may be empty)

A review is written against the order — not against the payment, and not against the person.

One order, one review. A UNIQUE constraint enforces it, so nobody can pad their own rating.

Complaints work differently. One order can raise several, because one bad delivery can go wrong in more than one way.

An admin may pick up a complaint. May — the column is allowed to be empty, because "nobody has looked at it yet" is a completely normal state. That empty-allowed column is exactly what the single line on the diagram means.

REVIEWCOMPLAINT SALE_ORDERADMIN_STAFF
STOP 10System-wide

Two things that run alongside the whole story

prices CROP+ VIRTUAL_ARAT+ ADMIN_STAFF→ DAILY_MARKET_PRICE
bazars PHYSICAL_BAZAR→ BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD→ CROP
alerts USERS→NOTIFICATION

Price reference is what makes the arat virtual — and notifications are what make the specialization pay off.

Prices. Admins log a price for each crop, at each arat, on each day. Those three together form the key, so the same price cannot be entered twice.

Bazars. PHYSICAL_BAZAR tracks real wet markets. BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD holds one day's trading at one bazar. A date on its own identifies nothing, so this is the second weak entity — same test as the storage unit.

Notifications. NOTIFICATION points straight at USERS, not at any role. All five roles get notified the same way, and USERS is the one table all five are guaranteed to be in. That only works because the specialization is total.

DAILY_MARKET_PRICEPHYSICAL_BAZAR BAZAR_DAILY_RECORDNOTIFICATION CROPVIRTUAL_ARATADMIN_STAFF USERS
Construct 4b — weak entity: BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD

Both weak entities pass the same one-line test, and it is worth saying out loud: take the owner away — does the key still identify anything?

"Unit 1" does not. "14 March" does not. So both carry their owner's key inside their own, and both are deleted when the owner is.

Part two

The graded constructs, instance by instance

The same five decisions, collected for quick reference. Each one opens with the test we applied — the actual question you ask to decide whether the construct belongs — then what we drew, the alternative that lost, and where in the running database the decision is enforced. Two of the tests can be worked out on paper in front of an examiner.

01

Total, disjoint specialization

USERS → FARMER · BUYER · ADMIN_STAFF · STORAGE_MANAGER · TRANSPORT_PERSONNEL

The test we applied

Is the missing value unknown, or is it inapplicable?

TradeLicenseNo on a farmer is not unknown — it does not apply. A NULL that means "does not apply" is a modelling smell; an absent row is honest. That alone justifies splitting the subclasses out.

The second reason is enforcement. On one merged table, you cannot write UQ_FARMER_NID at all: four fifths of the rows have a legitimately empty NID, so the column cannot be NOT NULL, and the rule "every farmer has an NID" disappears entirely. Separate tables let each role keep its own rules.

All five subclasses — what each one actually earns

SubclassHolds onlyThe rule it makes possible
FARMERNID, bank account, mobile banking, years farmingUQ_FARMER_NID — one national ID, one farmer.
BUYERBusiness name, buyer type, trade licenceUQ_BUYER_LICENSE + a CHECK limiting buyer type to four values.
ADMIN_STAFFEmployee ID, designationUQ_ADMIN_EMPLOYEEID, and a target for DAILY_MARKET_PRICE.LoggedBy.
STORAGE_MANAGEREmployee IDThird leg of the STORES ternary — a plain USERS FK could not say "must be a manager".
TRANSPORT_PERSONNELDriving licence, years drivingUQ_PERSONNEL_LICENSE, and third leg of ASSIGNED_TO.

Note the last two rows: STORAGE_MANAGER and TRANSPORT_PERSONNEL hold almost nothing, and someone will ask why they exist at all. The answer is that a ternary's third leg has to point somewhere — an FK to USERS would let a farmer be recorded as approving a storage allocation. The subclass is what makes the role a type, not just a label.

What we drew

An ISA triangle from USERS carrying a d circle for disjoint, on a double line for total. Five subclass boxes, each holding only its own attributes.

In the relational mapping each subclass PK doubles as its FK to the parent, so no extra key is invented and the 1:1 is structural.

Why not the alternatives

Single table + role flag: the role-specific uniqueness constraints become unwritable, and most columns sit NULL by design.

Five independent tables, no parent: email uniqueness across all users becomes impossible to enforce, and address/login columns are duplicated five times.

Enforced by

  • CK_USERS_ROLERestricts Role to exactly the five subclass names.
  • FK_FARMER_USERS…and four siblings — each subclass PK is also an FK to USERS.
  • Txn #1Registration writes USERS + subclass + phones atomically, so totality holds (BR-02).
02

Two recursive relationships

VIRTUAL_ARAT.supervises · BID.outbids

The test we applied

Do both ends of this relationship come from the same entity set?

A parent arat and a child arat are both arats. An outbidding bid and an outbid bid are both bids. When that is true the diamond has to loop back to the box it came from — there is nowhere else for it to go. But the two are recursive for different reasons, and they are different shapes, so each needs its own defence.

Instance A · a tree
VIRTUAL_ARAT.ParentAratID

Why recursive: the arat network is a chain of command — national, divisional, district. Every level is the same kind of thing, so they cannot be separate entities without repeating the same attributes three times.

What it buys: depth becomes data. Add an upazila tier and you insert rows; the schema does not move. A table per level would make that a migration.

Shape: 1:N — one parent, many children. Walked with CONNECT BY NOCYCLE. Exactly one row has a NULL parent: the root.

Instance B · a chain
BID.PreviousBidID

Why recursive: being outbid is a relationship between two bids. There is no third party involved, and no other entity it could point at.

What it buys — and this is the part to say: timestamps give you the order of bids, but not the displacement. Two bids on different batches are ordered in time and completely unrelated. PreviousBidID records that this bid beat that one, which is exactly what the OUTBID status change means. Without it, the status is a claim with no evidence behind it.

Shape: 1:1 — each bid displaces exactly one predecessor. It also survives two bids sharing a timestamp, which a time-ordered scan does not.

What we drew

A diamond with both legs returning to the same entity box, each leg labelled with the role it plays — parent / child for arats, outbids / outbid by for bids.

supervises is 1:N (one parent, many children). outbids is 1:1 — a chain, not a tree.

Why not the alternatives

A table per hierarchy level: freezes the depth of the arat network into the schema. Adding an upazila tier would mean a migration instead of a row.

A separate OUTBID junction table: strictly 1:1 with BID and carrying no attributes of its own — pure overhead.

Enforced by

  • CK_ARAT_NOT_OWN_PARENTAn arat cannot be its own parent (BR-22).
  • CONNECT BY NOCYCLETraverses the hierarchy and survives a longer cycle if one ever appears.
  • CK_BID_NOT_OWN_PREVIOUSA bid cannot outbid itself; BR-25 keeps the chain within one batch.
03

Two ternary relationships

STORES (batch × unit × manager) · ASSIGNED_TO (request × vehicle × personnel)

The test we applied — and it has a proof

Can the fact be split into binary relationships and put back together without loss?

Take a true situation: batch 7 goes into unit A approved by manager X, and into unit B approved by manager Y. Now split it into two ordinary relationships and rejoin them.

split  stored_in   = { (7, A), (7, B) }
         approved_by = { (7, X), (7, Y) }

rejoin (7, A, X) ✓   (7, A, Y) ✗
       (7, B, X) ✗   (7, B, Y) ✓

Four rows come back, and two of them are false. The information was destroyed, not merely made awkward to query. That is a lossy decomposition, and it is the formal proof that this relationship is irreducibly ternary.

The counter-test matters just as much. If every batch only ever had one manager, the manager would be determined by the batch, the rejoin would come back clean, and a ternary would be over-modelling. It is justified here precisely because none of the three legs is fixed by the other two.

Instance A · accountability
STORES — batch × unit × manager

The fact: this batch is in this unit because this manager approved it. The approval is not metadata about the placement — it is part of what the placement is.

Lose the ternary and you lose: which manager signed for which placement, the moment a batch is split across two units.

Cardinality: a batch may have several allocations over its life (pre-sale and post-sale), a unit holds many batches, a manager approves many. All three legs are many.

Instance B · responsibility
ASSIGNED_TO — request × vehicle × personnel

The fact: this driver drove this truck on this trip. Same three-way shape, and the same decomposition test fails the same way:

trip 3 → truck A, driver X
trip 3 → truck B, driver Y

rejoined → also yields (3, A, Y) and (3, B, X)

One real difference from STORES: a trip has at most one active assignment, while a truck and a driver each accumulate many. So the rule that blocks a duplicate is UQ_ASSIGNED_TRIPLE on all three columns — not the primary key, which is only a surrogate.

What we drew

One diamond with three lines leaving it, each to a different entity. Attributes of the event itself — quantity, dates, agreed rate, status — hang off the diamond, not off any participant.

Both map to a junction table with three foreign-key sets, a surrogate key, and a UNIQUE on the combination that must not repeat.

Why not the alternatives

Decomposing into binaries loses the connection between the legs. Two batch–unit rows and two batch–manager rows cannot tell you which manager authorised which placement — the information is destroyed, not just made harder to query.

Making the third party an attribute (a plain ManagerID column on a binary relationship) gets the same columns but drops the FK's meaning: the diagram would stop claiming the manager is a participant.

Enforced by

  • UQ_STORES_ALLOCATION(BatchID, WarehouseID, UnitNo, DateIn) — BR-08.
  • UQ_ASSIGNED_TRIPLE(TransportID, VehicleID, PersonnelID) — no duplicate assignment.
  • 3 FK sets eachIncluding the composite (WarehouseID, UnitNo) into the weak entity.
04

Two weak entities

STORAGE_UNIT ⊂ WAREHOUSE · BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD ⊂ PHYSICAL_BAZAR

The test we applied

Can this thing be identified by its own attributes alone?

Not "can it exist without its parent" — that is the answer most people give, and it is the wrong test. A FARM cannot exist without a farmer either, and a farm is a strong entity. Existence dependency is everywhere; it decides nothing.

FARM           “FarmID 7”  →  exactly one farm   → strong
STORAGE_UNIT  “unit 1”    →  five rows, one per warehouse  → weak

So UnitNo is not a key. It is a partial key — it separates units inside one warehouse, not across the database. The full key has to borrow the owner's: (WarehouseID, UnitNo).

Three things then follow as consequences, not as separate decisions: participation must be total (you cannot insert a unit without a warehouse — half its key would be missing); the identifying relationship must be 1:N from the owner (two owners would make the key ambiguous); and the delete rule must be CASCADE, because once the warehouse is gone the unit's identity is gone with it.

Instance A · a counter
STORAGE_UNIT ⊂ WAREHOUSE

Partial key: UnitNo — a number the warehouse assigns itself, restarting at 1 each time. trg_storage_unit_no deliberately does not use a global sequence.

Why not a global UnitID: it would work, but it models something false — nobody in the building recognises a globally unique unit number. And you would still need UNIQUE(WarehouseID, UnitNo) to stop duplicates, so you would carry the composite key and a surrogate. The composite was already doing the job.

Instance B · a date
BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD ⊂ PHYSICAL_BAZAR

Partial key: RecordDate — and this is a different flavour worth naming. UnitNo is a number somebody assigns; a date is a value that simply occurs. Neither is unique on its own, which is all the test asks.

“14 March” → one row per bazar → weak

Why not a surrogate RecordID: the natural key already exists and is already unique per bazar. Adding a number would let the same bazar log 14 March twice — the composite key prevents that for free.

A simplification to own, not hide

The key is (BazarID, RecordDate) while CropID is an ordinary column — so a bazar can record only one crop per day. Real wet markets trade many. PHYSICAL_BAZAR and BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD are P2 scope (seeded, no UI), and the honest fix is to widen the key to (BazarID, RecordDate, CropID).

Naming this yourself turns a spotted flaw into a demonstrated understanding of what the key actually means.

What we drew

A double box joined by a double diamond to its owner, on a double line — participation in the identifying relationship is always total, by definition. The partial key is dash-underlined: UnitNo, RecordDate.

Mapped as a composite PK that begins with the owner's key.

Why not the alternatives

A global UnitID sequence would make the unit strong — and would be a lie about the world. Every warehouse numbers its units from 1; a globally unique unit number is not a thing anyone in the building would recognise. trg_storage_unit_no assigns the next number per warehouse.

ON DELETE CASCADE, not RESTRICT: a weak entity has no meaning once its owner is gone, so orphan prevention is not enough — the rows must go with it.

Enforced by

  • PK_STORAGE_UNIT(WarehouseID, UnitNo) — owner key first.
  • PK_BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD(BazarID, RecordDate) — same shape.
  • ON DELETE CASCADEOn both FK_UNIT_WAREHOUSE and FK_BDR_BAZAR.
05

Two aggregations

(BUYER–BID–HARVEST_BATCH) → creates → SALE_ORDER · STORES → settled by → STORAGE_PAYMENT

The test we applied

Is the thing being pointed at the relationship itself, or one of its participants?

Start from the rule, because it is mechanical: in Chen notation a relationship cannot be the endpoint of another relationship. A diamond may only touch boxes. So aggregation is not a pattern you pick — it is the only legal way to draw a situation that genuinely occurs. The only judgement is whether it occurs.

Instance A · the clean case
STORES → settled by → STORAGE_PAYMENT

What is the fee paid for? Not the batch — the same batch can be stored twice and is billed separately each time. Not the unit — a unit is a shelf, it does not generate a bill. Not the manager.

It is paid for the allocation: this batch, in this unit, from this date, at this rate, approved by this manager. The allocation is the ternary. Diamond cannot touch diamond, so the box is forced.

Lead with this one. There is no alternative design — you cannot point the payment at any single participant without losing information.

Instance B · the arguable case
(BUYER–BID–HARVEST_BATCH) → creates → SALE_ORDER

Expect the pushback: "BID has its own key and its own attributes, so it is an entity — just draw a binary line to SALE_ORDER."

That produces identical DDL and is a legitimate alternative. Concede it, then say why ours claims more: a bid's whole meaning is the buyer-batch-price pairing, so what causes the order is the complete bidding event, not a standalone record.

Conceding that the alternative works reads as command of the material. Insisting it is wrong reads as rehearsed.

The fingerprint in the tables. Aggregation is why STORES carries a surrogate AllocationID instead of just a composite of its three foreign keys. Giving a relationship its own key is precisely what makes it referenceable — so a relationship table with a surrogate key that something else points at is an aggregation in the relational mapping.

What we drew

A dashed box enclosing a relationship, with a further diamond attached to the box's edge. It is the notation's answer to a single question: what do you do when a relationship needs to participate in another relationship?

Both aggregations map the same way — the enclosed relationship gets a surrogate key, and the outer relationship's table carries that key as a foreign key.

Why not the alternatives

Diamond touching diamond is simply not legal Chen notation, and an examiner will spot it immediately. The dashed box exists precisely so you never have to.

A binary BID → SALE_ORDER relationship yields identical DDL but a weaker claim — it says the bid alone explains the order. The aggregation is the model asserting something true that the foreign key alone cannot express.

Enforced by

  • UQ_ORDER_BIDOne sale order per winning bid, strictly 1:1 (BR-16).
  • PK_STORESAllocationID — the surrogate that makes the ternary referenceable.
  • FK_STORAGE_PAYMENT_ALLOCThe payment points at the allocation, not at the batch or the unit.
Say this before you are asked

On the printed diagram the STORES aggregation box encloses the diamond and its attributes, but the three participating entities sit outside it — they are too far apart on the sheet to enclose without redrawing the right half of the model.

Strictly, the box should contain its participants. Naming that yourself, once, costs nothing and closes the question: "the box marks the ternary as one aggregate fact; the three legs cross its boundary for layout reasons."

The supporting constructs

4 derived attributes · 4 composite keys · 1 multivalued attribute

Every remaining instance, so nothing is left unexplained

These are not graded separately, but each one is a decision an examiner can point at.

Two rules cover all of them. A value that can be computed is never stored — a stored copy is a copy that can go stale. And a key is composite when the combination is the identity — adding a surrogate on top would let the same fact be recorded twice.

Derived attributes — dashed ovals

All four are GENERATED ALWAYS AS … VIRTUAL, so Oracle computes them on read and they cannot disagree with their inputs.

HARVEST_BATCH.AvailableQuantity
  = Total − Reserved − Sold

SALE_ORDER.TotalAmount
  = AcceptedQuantity × AcceptedPricePerKg

STORES.StorageFee
  = QuantityStored × FeePerKgSnapshot

STORES.MinimumReleaseDate
  = DateIn + MinimumStorageDays

Why StorageFee uses a snapshot rate rather than reading the warehouse's current rate: the fee must reflect what was agreed on the day, not what the manager charges today. Virtual columns may only read their own row — which here is exactly the behaviour we want.

Age is deliberately not one of these. It would need SYSDATE, and a virtual column must be deterministic. It is computed in a view instead.

Composite keys — and why each one is composite

USER_PHONE (UserID, PhoneNo) — the multivalued attribute. A column holds one value, so several phone numbers need several rows. The pair is the identity; a PhoneID would allow the same number twice for one user.

STORAGE_UNIT (WarehouseID, UnitNo) — weak entity, owner's key first. Covered above.

BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD (BazarID, RecordDate) — weak entity, same shape.

DAILY_MARKET_PRICE (CropID, AratID, PriceDate) — and this one is not a weak entity, which is the distinction worth drawing. All three parts are keys of strong entities in their own right. Nothing is borrowing anyone's identity; the triple simply is the fact. That composite gives BR-21 — one price per crop, per arat, per day — with no trigger and no application check.

The distinction to keep straight

  • weakComposite key because part of it is borrowed from an owner. Implies total participation and cascade delete.
  • compositeComposite key because the combination is the identity. No owner, no cascade, no weakness.
Part three

Total vs partial participation

The single line / double line distinction is the easiest mark to lose and the easiest to defend, because in this model it maps almost perfectly onto one thing: whether the foreign key column is NOT NULL.

Total — double line

Every instance of this entity must take part. The FK is NOT NULL.

  • FARM → FARMER — land without an owner is not in the system.
  • HARVEST_BATCH → FARM, CROP, VIRTUAL_ARAT — all three required to list a batch.
  • STORAGE_UNIT → WAREHOUSE — automatic: weak entities are always total.
  • SALE_ORDER → the bid aggregation — no order without a winning bid.
  • TRANSPORT_REQUEST → SALE_ORDER — NOT NULL and UNIQUE, so 1:1 total.
  • PAYMENT → SALE_ORDER, BUYER, FARMER — money always has all three ends.
  • NOTIFICATION → USERS — a notification with no recipient is meaningless.

Partial — single line

Some instances take part, some never do. The FK is nullable, or lives on the other side.

  • BID → SALE_ORDER — most bids lose, and stay valid bids.
  • HARVEST_BATCH → STORES — plenty of batches go straight to auction.
  • SALE_ORDER → REVIEW — reviewing is optional, always.
  • COMPLAINT → ADMIN_STAFF — HandledByAdminID is nullable; unclaimed is a real state.
  • VEHICLE → ASSIGNED_TO — a vehicle in maintenance is assigned to nothing.
  • VIRTUAL_ARAT → itself — the root arat has no parent. ParentAratID is NULL exactly once.
The one honest exception — worth volunteering

The specialization is total on the diagram, but no single constraint enforces it. CK_USERS_ROLE restricts which role a user may claim; nothing in the DDL forces the matching subclass row to exist. It is guaranteed by the registration transaction, which inserts USERS, the subclass row and the phone rows together and rolls back all of them on any failure.

Being the one to point this out is a stronger position than being asked. The correct follow-up answer: enforcing it declaratively would need a deferred assertion, which Oracle 11g does not support.

Part five

Questions you should expect

Eleven questions an examiner is likely to ask about this diagram, with the short answer for each. Every one of them has been checked against the running database rather than the original design document.

Why is there no diamond directly between HARVEST_BATCH and STORAGE_UNIT?

There is — it is STORES. The manager is a third participant in that same diamond, not a separate relationship. A batch is not simply "in a unit"; it is in a unit because a named manager allocated it there, and that authorisation is part of the same fact.

Adding a second, binary batch–unit diamond alongside it would duplicate what the ternary already asserts while claiming something weaker.

Can two relationship diamonds connect to each other directly?

No. In Chen notation a relationship connects entities, and only entities. When a relationship genuinely needs to participate in another relationship — as STORES does with settled by — the notation's answer is aggregation: enclose it in a dashed box and treat the box as a participant.

This is precisely why both aggregations exist in this model, and it is the shortest correct answer to "why is that box dashed?"

Why remove the AUCTION entity? Auctions are central to your system.

The activity is central; the entity was not. An AUCTION would have been in strict 1:1 with HARVEST_BATCH, with no key of its own and no ability to exist independently — three timing attributes and a minimum price in a box.

Merging them onto the batch removes a mandatory join from the most frequent query in the application (browse open listings) and removes a class of bug where a batch exists with no auction row. Bidding is still fully modelled — it just lives on BID and HARVEST_BATCH.

Why is SALE_ORDER attached to an aggregation instead of just to BID?

Because the foreign key would be identical either way, but the claim would not. A binary BID → SALE_ORDER says the bid alone explains the order. What actually explains it is the combination — this buyer, on this batch, at this price — and that combination is the relationship, not any one entity.

This is the case where the ER diagram carries meaning the DDL cannot, which is exactly what aggregation is for.

Why is STORAGE_UNIT weak rather than strong with its own ID?

Apply the test: remove the warehouse and ask whether UnitNo = 3 still identifies anything. It does not — every warehouse numbers its own units from 1, so there are many "unit 3"s. That is the definition of a partial key.

A global UnitID would make it strong, but would model something nobody in the warehouse would recognise. trg_storage_unit_no deliberately assigns numbers per warehouse rather than from a global sequence.

Why keep two separate payment entities?

Different payer, different payee, different rules. PAYMENT is buyer → farmer and is governed by BR-19 (may not exceed the order total) and BR-20 (only after delivery). STORAGE_PAYMENT is farmer-or-buyer → warehouse and is governed by neither.

Merging them behind a type flag would put the storage fee under a trigger written for sale money — two independent rule sets entangled in one place, for the sake of one fewer table.

Is your specialization really total? What enforces it?

It is total in the model and in practice, but it is the one construct not enforced by a single declarative constraint. CK_USERS_ROLE restricts which of the five roles a user may hold; nothing forces the matching subclass row to exist.

The guarantee comes from the registration transaction, which writes the parent row, the subclass row and the phone rows together and rolls back all of them on any failure. A declarative version would need a deferred assertion, which Oracle 11g does not support.

Why does BID need PreviousBidID? Timestamps already give you the order.

Timestamps tell you the sequence; they do not tell you the displacement. Two bids on different batches are ordered by time but unrelated. PreviousBidID records that this specific bid displaced that specific one, which is what the OUTBID status change actually means.

It also makes the history a single hierarchical query instead of a windowed scan, and it survives the case where two bids share a timestamp.

Why is CROP_CATEGORY a separate entity? It only has five rows.

Because it carries an attribute of its own. Hold the category on CROP as plain columns and you get CropID → CategoryName → Description — a transitive dependency on a non-key attribute, which is a 3NF violation. The description belongs to the category, not to the crop.

The anomalies are concrete: renaming Cereal to Cereal Grains would have to touch every rice, wheat and maize row; a new category could not be created before some crop used it; and deleting the last oilseed crop would take the category's definition with it. Row count is not the test — dependency is.

And note we did not do this everywhere. BuyerType, QualityGrade and AllocationStatus are all CHECK constraints, not tables, because each is nothing but a label from a closed set. The rule we applied: a classification becomes an entity when it carries attributes of its own; otherwise it stays a CHECK constraint.

Why does NOTIFICATION reference USERS instead of a role?

Because all five roles get notified in exactly the same way, with the same columns. It is the one place in the schema where the superclass is the right target — and it only works because the specialization is total: every recipient, whatever their role, is guaranteed to have a USERS row.

What would you change if you drew it again?

Two things, both cosmetic rather than structural. First, lay the right-hand side out so the STORES aggregation box can enclose all three of its participants instead of just the diamond. Second, place REVIEW beside COMPLAINT from the start — both attach to SALE_ORDER, and drawing them apart invited exactly one wrong connector, which is how it was originally drawn to PAYMENT before being corrected.

Structurally, the five graded constructs would all be modelled the same way again.
