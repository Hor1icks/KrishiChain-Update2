KrishiChain — Relational Schema

Mapped from the Chen E-R model. Twenty-eight relations, forty-one foreign keys, on Oracle 11g XE. CSE-302 Database Management Systems · Group A6, Military Institute of Science and Technology.

Relational Schema Diagram

Twenty-eight relations. Arrows point from foreign-key attributes to the primary keys they reference, matching the textbook schema convention. Every relation and column below was checked against database/01_create_tables.sql.

Underlined row primary keyPK, FK both key typesArrow direction FK to referenced PKUQ unique constraintvirtual Oracle generated column, not stored
Mapping notes: Name and Address are flattened into their parts. Multivalued PhoneNo becomes USER_PHONE. Derived attributes become Oracle virtual columns (AvailableQuantity, TotalAmount, StorageFee, MinimumReleaseDate); Age is left to the query layer. The specialization becomes parent plus five subclass tables whose primary key is also a foreign key to USERS. Both weak entities take the owner key plus their partial key. Both ternaries become tables carrying three foreign key sets plus their own relationship attributes, with a UNIQUE across the three so a triple cannot repeat. The first aggregation is realised by SALE_ORDER referencing BID with a UNIQUE, which is what makes it one order per winning bid. The second is realised by STORES carrying its own surrogate key AllocationID, which STORAGE_PAYMENT then references. Oracle 11g has no IDENTITY column, so every surrogate key is a sequence plus a BEFORE INSERT trigger.
Schema verified against the live Oracle 11g XE database — 28 relations, 41 foreign keys, 19 sequences, 22 triggers. PricePerKg and LoggedBy on BAZAR_DAILY_RECORD, and CropID in its primary key, are the pending migration and are shown here as the target shape.
